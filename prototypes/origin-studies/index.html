One seed, three ways of drawing it.

The same seven-stage story, from a single form to a living system, rendered in three different visual languages. Each study below is a live prototype of the first two stages, Origin and Awakening. Drag the line to scrub, or press Play.

Step 1 · The current implementation

What stays, what changes.

Read from the live code: the scroll engine in StoryPlayer.tsx, the axonometric drawing in plate.ts, the beat timeline in beats.ts, and the site's styles.

Keep

  • The scroll engine. A sticky stage inside a tall track; scroll position maps straight to a timeline, followed with 90 ms of smoothing. It never intercepts scrolling and renders only while on screen.
  • Stateless frames. Every frame is a pure function of the timeline position. The new renderer keeps that contract, so scrubbing backwards, deep links and stills all keep working.
  • Captions as real text, with the Gold progress rule and chapter ticks. The animation never carries meaning on its own.
  • The fallbacks. Reduced motion and short screens get one still per stage, drawn by the same renderer; without JavaScript the words stand alone. The skip link stays.
  • The build switch and checks (STORY_PREVIEW, check-story), so the story can still be turned off and verified.
  • The page ground, the Ivory to Travertine gradient, as the environment every direction grows in.

Redesign

  • The renderer. The 2D axonometric canvas drawing (plate.ts) is replaced by an organic 3D renderer. This is where the work is.
  • The timeline. 44 beats in 8 chapters become 7 stages with fewer, longer transformations, so it reads as one continuous growth instead of a sequence of steps.
  • The words. Stage titles and captions move from brand-building steps (grid, mark, palette) to the seven-stage growth story, still naming what each stage means for a brand.
  • The length. About 10 viewports today. Seven stages fit in about 6 to 7, which keeps the pin from feeling like being held.

Decisions this needs

  • Rendering technology. Recommended: raw WebGL with a signed-distance shader (about 10–15 KB, no library). Alternative: three.js (about 150 KB compressed), only if the approved direction needs mesh-based tools.
  • Colours beyond the five. Your brief allows a few earthy additions. They would appear only inside the artwork, never in text, buttons or layout, and need sign-off per direction below.
  • Brand rules. The design system and brand skill rule out gradients, shadows, heavy 3D and particles. Your brief overrides them for the story artwork only; I'd record that as a named exception.
  • Performance on phones. 3D shading costs GPU time. The plan: adaptive resolution, render only while visible, and stills on low-power devices.

A

Kiln

The idea as a made object. A matte ceramic seed, irregular and hand-formed, opens along gold seams to reveal a burnished heart, and the first growth rises from it.

Shape and form

Soft, fully closed volumes with a hand-formed irregularity: no perfect ellipses, no sharp facets. Parts meet through smooth fillets, the way clay is joined, so growth looks fused rather than attached.

Material and texture

Matte stoneware with a fine mineral grain for the shell; a satin clay glaze on the inside faces; a single burnished gold for the heart and seams. Tendrils in a waxy, matte olive.

Lighting

One soft key from the upper left, like a north-light studio, with ambient occlusion in the folds and a soft cast shadow on the ground. The gold catches a warm reflection of the room.

Motion

Slow, weighted movement. The seed condenses rather than scales in; the shells part like a pod drying open; tendrils grow from their base with a gentle sway. The camera drifts a few degrees, never orbits.

References to look at

  • Barbara Hepworth and Isamu Noguchi: pierced, hand-finished organic sculpture
  • Hans Coper and Lucie Rie: matte stoneware surfaces and proportion
  • Kintsugi: gold that marks where a form opened
  • Seed pods and husks splitting as they dry
  1. 01OriginA matte ceramic seed condenses out of the ground, slightly irregular, its gold seams only just visible.
  2. 02AwakeningThe shell parts along its seams into three lobes; the gold heart contracts into a core and the first tendrils rise from it.
  3. 03FoundationBelow, roots grow as matte ceramic tubes that branch and fuse into a woven base, casting soft shadows across the ground.
  4. 04ExpansionFive branches reach out as unequal, hand-thrown forms, each joined to the core by a smooth fillet, never a node-and-line diagram.
  5. 05DifferentiationEach branch takes its own glaze and surface: satin clay, raw stone, olive, fine crackle, one burnished bronze.
  6. 06IdentityThe lobes close around the branches into one vessel-like sculpture, and the gold seams join into a single continuous line.
  7. 07EcosystemSmaller forms bud from the base and settle around it, linked by fine gold threads, as the camera steps back to show the field.

B

Mineral

The idea as something that accretes. A frosted, translucent stone, lit from within by gold veins, grows in rings and unfolds translucent blades from its crown.

Shape and form

Melted, pebble-like lobes that grow by accretion, layer over layer, like agate or travertine itself. New growth is thin and blade-like, so the mass of the seed and the lightness of the growth contrast.

Material and texture

Frosted, semi-translucent stone: light passes through thin edges and is absorbed in thick ones. Growth bands show on the surface. Fine gold veins sit inside the body, visible through it.

Lighting

Back-lit, so the form glows at its edges against the ground, with a soft frosted highlight in front. The shadow on the ground is lighter and slightly tinted, as light passes through.

Motion

Growth by accumulation: rings spread outward through the stone, veins brighten, blades unfold and lean out with a slow curl. Movement is continuous and slower than A.

References to look at

  • Agate and travertine cross-sections: growth recorded as bands
  • Frosted and cast glass sculpture: light held inside a mass
  • Ernst Haeckel's radiolaria plates, read for structure rather than detail
  • Coral and mineral deposits growing by accretion
  1. 01OriginA frosted pebble of sage stone takes shape, lit faintly from within by gold veins.
  2. 02AwakeningGrowth rings spread outward through the stone and translucent blades unfold from its crown.
  3. 03FoundationRoots spread below like soft mineral deposits, the gold veins running down through them into the ground.
  4. 04ExpansionThe blades branch into translucent fans that overlap; where they overlap, light passes through several layers.
  5. 05DifferentiationEach fan settles into its own density and tone: pale sage, deep forest, warm grey, a touch of smoky rose.
  6. 06IdentityThe layers align so every vein meets at one point; the whole reads as a single stone with one light inside.
  7. 07EcosystemSmaller stones accrete around it, linked by glowing veins along the ground.

C

Filament

The idea as a living thread. A seed wound from hundreds of fine filaments breathes, loosens and unwinds into roots and shoots, while spores of gold drift free.

Shape and form

No solid surfaces: volume is built from many fine lines wound around a seed-shaped core, like a cocoon or mycelium. Depth comes from line weight and tone, nearer threads darker and heavier.

Material and texture

Silk and fibre: satin threads with a slight sheen, spores as small points of gold. The most graphic of the three, closest to drawing, but drawn in space.

Lighting

Atmospheric rather than modelled: distance fades threads towards Travertine, the nearest threads carry Espresso. A gold glint marks every growing tip.

Motion

Procedural and generative: threads draw themselves in, the cocoon breathes, loosens and unwinds along flow lines. The most alive of the three and the cheapest to render.

References to look at

  • Silk cocoons and woven basketry

  • Flow-field generative drawing
  1. 01OriginHundreds of fine threads wind themselves into a seed-shaped cocoon, which begins to breathe.
  2. 02AwakeningThe threads loosen; some unwind into roots below and shoots above, and spores of gold drift free.
  3. 03FoundationThe roots spread into a mesh, threads meeting and splitting across the ground.
  4. 04ExpansionShoots braid into bundles that reach into space, one bundle for each dimension of the brand.
  5. 05DifferentiationEach bundle changes weight, colour and weave: a tight espresso braid, loose moss, bronze, smoky rose.
  6. 06IdentityThe bundles twist back together into one woven pattern, recognisable as a single strand.
  7. 07EcosystemThe weave reaches beyond the frame, and new cocoons begin to form at its ends.

Recommendation

A · Kiln, with Mineral's gold veins as the Differentiation detail.

Kiln speaks the brand's own materials: stone, ceramic, gold, olive. It has real volume without looking like a 3D showcase, and it stays crisp on phones. Borrowing the inner gold veins from Mineral gives Stage 05 its moment of individuality without the translucency risk.

Directions can also be mixed: for example Kiln's forms with Filament's roots for the Foundation stage. Nothing on the live site has changed. Once a direction is approved, the full seven-stage animation replaces the current renderer inside the existing scroll engine.

Prototypes cover Stages 01 and 02 only. Colours marked "Addition" are outside the five CALIX colours and are proposed for the artwork only. References are named rather than shown, to avoid reproducing other artists' work.
