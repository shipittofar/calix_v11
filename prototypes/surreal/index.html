Real objects in surreal scenes · 14 November 2026

The Seed.

One real seed, treated like the most precious object in the world. It is studied, measured, set into a foundation, cast in gold, turned into a mark, and carried into every place a brand lives. It never sprouts. Like the pear in your reference, it is the single object the visitor follows from the first frame to the last.

1 · Analysis

What the two references tell us.

The current site

  • Keep: Cormorant and Jost, the five colours, the Ivory ground, the outline header button, calm short copy, and the scroll engine already built (pinned acts, wipes, push-ins, masked headlines, static fallbacks).
  • What failed: abstract forms (a drawing, a stone, a sculpture) that carried no nameable meaning, and contrast that was too even.

The reference imagery

  • Real, nameable objects: a pear, a drape, scaffolding, a knife, a mosaic, a classical figure.
  • Surreal by scale and context, with art-historical treatments and strong light against dark.
  • One object carries the story. The pear grows, is grafted, gilded, monumentalised and set in mosaic. We borrow that structure, not the pear or the scaffold.

2 · The concept

An idea, handled like a treasure.

The seed is the idea. Everything that happens to it is something a brand studio does: examine, structure, refine, mark, systematise, apply, connect. That is how the story stays about building a brand, not about nature.

The object

An acorn: unmistakably a seed, with a strong silhouette that can become a mark, and no olive association. Alternatives: a peach stone (sculptural, grooved) or an almond (elegant, but close to an olive pit).

The rule

The seed never sprouts, roots or grows into a tree. It is examined, cast, set, printed and carried, like a museum piece or a precious material.

The look

Medium-format photography and photomontage; one high window of light; espresso and olive shadows; ivory and travertine surfaces; antique gold on the seed and the mark only.

3 · Storyboard

Ten shots, one seed.

Composition sketches, not final images. Each shot names its real objects, the surreal turn, the light, the camera, where the words sit, and how it becomes the next shot. The label on each frame is the part of the home page it carries.
